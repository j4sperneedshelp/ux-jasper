# j01: Trying to find good sheet music
I took piano lessons for many years when I was younger but, unfortunately, I was not able to bring a keyboard with me to the dorms when I came to university, so I had to take a break from the hobby for several years. After moving out of the dorms and into an apartment, I was able to get a keyboard and resume my hobby. Since I was no longer taking lessons and had no teacher to provide sheet music for me, I had to find a suitable arrangement myself whenever I wanted to learn a new song. One of the websites that I found most useful in this endeavor was musescore.com, which provides free user uploaded arrangements for many popular songs and allows you to hear what the notes printed on the sheet music will actually sound like in your browser. The large library of available songs and the ability to hear a playback of the song in your browser make musescore a very useful tool for finding sheet music.  Recently, I decided that the next song I wanted to learn was "House of the Rising Sun" (specifically the version by the Animals), and started looking for piano sheet music on musescore. I found an arrangement that was specifically tagged as piano sheet music and that seemed relatively easy to play, and as I always do, I hit the "toggle play" button to hear what it would sound like in order to decide whether it was worth learning this version or if I should keep looking. What started to play on my speakers, however, was not just a piano arrangement, but an arrangement for an entire quintet, including a guitar, a harmonica, and drums. This was obviously not helpful at all, since I could barely hear what the piano part sounded like. I figured that there was surely a way to toggle which instruments I could hear the parts of, so I started scanning the interface for any button that looked like it would allow me to do so. I found a button (circled in red below) that I thought looked promising and clicked on it.

<img width="628" height="57" alt="Screenshot 2026-11-11 183815" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/dfd5890a-a6db-42aa-bad7-673992e59000" />

Surely enough, this button brought up a menu with exactly what I was looking for, a way to toggle the volume of all the instruments that were part of the quintet arrangement. Unfortunately, the slider toggles themselves were greyed out and unselectable, with a message at the top telling me to select the synthesizer option to use the mixer.

<img width="1461" height="767" alt="Screenshot 2026-11-11 180511" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e8054f8f-240e-4964-9957-e85f8ff2c8d9" />

I closed the menu and started looking around the interface to find the synthesizer option I was told to use. There were no buttons with the label "synthesizer" or any buttons with icons that resembled a synthesizer. There was, however, a menu on the side that looked like a drop down menu to change the instrument. I figured that maybe this was what the message was talking about, and scrolled down the drop down menu until I found the synthesizer option. 

<img width="657" height="555" alt="Screenshot 2026-11-11 180735" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d61170e6-c224-4154-a3da-455a2ea12910" />

Instead of allowing me to toggle the volume of the different instruments in the arrangement, this button sent me to a page with a completely different piece of sheet music, which I assume was supposed to be an arrangement of the same song for a synthesizer, but I couldn't tell because the page was almost completely blank. 

<img width="1916" height="712" alt="Screenshot 2026-11-11 180854" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/cd4a4462-6ef6-404a-ac00-2b7b46c69d59" />

I think this interaction is a great representation of a mismatch between my own **mental model** and the way the website was really designed. A mental model is the understanding a person develops about how a system works and what they expect to happen when they interact with it. After seeing the message that I had to select the synthesizer option, my expectation was that any button labeled "synthesizer" would allow me to toggle the instrument sliders, assuming that the creators of the website wouldn't create two buttons with the same label and completely separate functionalities. This expectation, however, did not line up with the way the website actually worked.


Ultimately, I was not able to figure out where the true "synthesizer" button was, and had to figure out what the piano part sounded like by taking a picture of the sheet music on my phone, getting up, walking over to my keyboard, and actually playing it myself. 


I think the easiest way for musescore to fix this issue would be to allow users to use the "mixer" feature without having to select the synthesizer option. If there is a technical reason for this not being possible, I would suggest that the option be renamed to something that isn't the name of an instrument, and that the button to select this option should be put somewhere easy to find, maybe next to the button to open the mixer menu.
